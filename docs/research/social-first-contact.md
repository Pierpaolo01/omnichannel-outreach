# Social first contact: LinkedIn, Instagram and Facebook

Research for [Verify first-contact options for LinkedIn, Instagram and Facebook](https://github.com/Pierpaolo01/omnichannel-outreach/issues/8). Checked **11 November 2026** against public first-party documentation. Social channels remain a later expansion. The settled MVP remains scraper CSV imports, campaigns, email/SMS, and a daily manual-phone call list. The user speaks, automation stops on prospect reply, and the user takes over appointments. InMail remains a candidate, not an adopted decision. Regulatory review and implementation architecture are outside this report.

## LinkedIn

**Ordinary messages and invitations are different actions.** Normal member messaging serves first-degree connections. Accepting a connection invitation creates that relationship. An invitation can carry a short note before acceptance, and the recipient can reply without accepting. Therefore an invitation sent, an invitation accepted, and a prospect reply are different events. Free accounts have limited personalized invitation notes; Premium removes the note-count limit, not every invitation restriction. Sources: [messaging FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550206), [invitation responses](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a540852), [personalized invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153).

**InMail reaches nonconnections without an accepted invitation.** The sender needs an eligible paid subscription and available credits. Recipient message preferences still apply: opting out removes the InMail option. LinkedIn does not offer unlimited InMail credits. A paid sender plan is not evidence that every profile is reachable. [InMail overview](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a543895).

Actual allowances and sending locations differ:

- Premium Career supplies 5 monthly credits, Premium Business 15, and Premium All-in-One 30. [InMail FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554515).
- Sales Navigator Core, Advanced and Advanced Plus each supply 50 monthly credits, with a maximum accumulated balance of 150. Extra credits cannot currently be purchased. Responses within 90 days can restore credits. LinkedIn says Sales Navigator InMails are sent through Sales Navigator rather than LinkedIn.com. [Sales Navigator credits](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a101030), [sending location and recipient settings](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a102025).
- Recruiter Lite supplies 30 monthly credits and permits additional purchases, but only reaches candidates through third-degree connections. Profiles beyond that network cannot be contacted through Lite InMail. Full Recruiter advertises broader network access and 100–150 monthly InMails. These are recruiting products, not interchangeable sales-plan entitlements. [Recruiter Lite FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a1675961), [Recruiter comparison](https://business.linkedin.com/in/en/hire/recruiter?ss=1).

**Open Profile is a separate free route.** A Premium recipient can enable it so other members, including free-account senders, can contact them without connecting or spending InMail credits. A Premium badge does not prove Open Profile is enabled. LinkedIn limits Open Profile sending, including monthly limits for Basic accounts, without publishing a universal count on these pages. Sales Navigator calls messages to Open Profiles or first-degree connections “free InMail.” That label does not mean a paid credit was consumed. [Open Profile settings](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a541684), [message types](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a1451572).

Other documented exceptions include message requests to coworkers and fellow Group members. These can be accepted or privately declined and are subject to limits. Thus “every nonconnection requires InMail” is false. [Message requests](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552485).

## Instagram

**Native messaging and API messaging have different entry conditions.** Instagram's consumer interface supports initiating messages, including to nonfollowers. Depending on recipient settings, the message becomes a request. Before acceptance, a new request can contain text only; photos, videos and voice notes are unavailable. Acceptance sends future messages to the inbox. Recipients can disable requests entirely. These rules also matter when a professional account is the sender; being a business does not establish recipient acceptance. [Who you can message](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/1435783229983256?locale=en_US), [sending messages](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/155540431448273?locale=en_US), [request controls](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/585369912141614?locale=en_US).

The official API serves professional accounts, meaning businesses and creators, rather than a personal consumer account acting as an API sender. Facebook Login requires a linked Facebook Page. Instagram Login and the newer Unified Login do not. Unified Login requires a business portfolio. These authentication differences do not grant permission to message an arbitrary profile. [Instagram Login](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/), [current setup comparison](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/).

The Instagram Send Messages guide requires the person to message the professional account before ordinary API sending. It uses an Instagram-scoped recipient ID from the interaction, an authorized token, and messaging permissions. The standard window is normally 24 hours; a person's first message after clicking a Click-to-Direct ad may open up to seven days. A separate Human Agent window permits human responses for seven days. These extensions still depend on interaction. They do not establish cold-contact eligibility. [Send Messages](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/messaging-api/).

**A comment can permit the first private reply without an earlier DM.** Meta documents one private reply to someone commenting on the professional account's post, reel, story, Live or ad post. Send within seven days; Live replies must occur during the broadcast. Nonfollowers receive the reply in Requests. Follow-up requires the recipient to respond, then falls within 24 hours of that response. The route uses the comment ID, not an arbitrary username. [Private replies](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/private-replies/).

## Facebook and Messenger

**Personal profiles can initiate contact with nonfriends.** The message may land in Message Requests, and blocking can prevent delivery. This native capability is different from sending as a Page or through the business API. [Who you can message](https://www.facebook.com/help/438779846195810?locale=en_US).

**Pages have a meaningful first-contact exception.** Meta's current Help Center says eligible Pages can send up to 10 new message requests daily to people and Pages, including followers and nonfollowers. Availability is gradual. Sender requirements include messaging enabled, an adult admin and supported country; recipient settings, age and country also matter. Receiving Pages must allow the sender through their audience controls and must not have blocked it. The article's later eligibility text still refers specifically to large follower bases and follower messaging. It supplies neither a numerical follower threshold nor a country list. This verifies a documented UI capability, not availability for the intended account or API support. [Page message requests](https://www.facebook.com/help/1518695669102556?locale=en_US).

Messenger's business API sends as a Page using Page authorization, messaging permissions and scoped recipient identifiers. Ordinary access depends on a qualifying user interaction. The documentation includes sending a message and clicking an in-conversation Get Started button; a prior typed DM is therefore not the only trigger. The usual window is 24 hours, with up to seven days after an ad-generated first message. A profile URL or an app-scoped Facebook Login user ID is not a substitute for Messenger recipient eligibility. [Messenger overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/overview/), [sending requirements and identifiers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/send-messages/).

Private replies provide another entry point: one message within seven days of an eligible Page visitor post or comment, with continued conversation dependent on the person responding. Meta also documents Group private replies. The Help Center limits Group availability to some public groups and Pages, requires group permission, excludes private groups and replies to other Pages, and retains the seven-day comment limit. [API private replies](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/discovery/private-replies/), [Group availability](https://www.facebook.com/help/3508700512732880?locale=en_US).

Paid marketing messaging is another official exception to ordinary response windows, but requires opted-in subscribers, approved tech-provider access and supported regions. The current API excludes EU recipients. It does not establish a cold-message route from a scraped profile. [Marketing Message API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/marketing-messages-on-messenger/).

## Implications and evidence limits

The later product choice must distinguish native first-contact requests, connection-dependent messages, paid InMail and interaction-triggered API replies. A social profile URL identifies a target; it establishes none of those prerequisites. Connection acceptance and message-request acceptance also differ from an actual prospect reply, which remains the agreed human-takeover trigger.

An authenticated-browser action would use the sender's native account capability. It would not prove API access or supported automation. LinkedIn's documented Messages API is restricted to approved partners and describes first-degree messaging and existing-conversation replies. Purchasing Premium alone does not establish that access. [LinkedIn Messages API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/integrations/communications/messages).

All platform claims above come from official public documents. Meta developer pages returned 429 errors through the web fetcher; unauthenticated HTTP requests with `Accept: text/markdown` retrieved their content. Help Center article bodies were retrieved through public HTML, including embedded article JSON, without login. No account, browser session, credentials or live sending capability was tested. Country rollout, actual subscription balances, recipient settings, app approval and intended-account capability remain unverified. Meta's general messaging overview lists comment interactions broadly; the dedicated private-reply pages impose the narrower one-message and recipient-response conditions reported here.
